01 / 07 · 0 – 39,000 ft Troposphere

Through the weather.

Almost every cloud, storm and raindrop lives in this first layer, along with most of the air on Earth. You rise straight through it, smoothly, at about the speed of a bicycle.

02 / 07 · ≈ 35,000 ft Cruising altitude

Above the airliners.

Passenger jets level off around here. You keep climbing, to nearly three times their height.

03 / 07 · ≈ 39,000 ft Tropopause

Where the weather ends.

The air stops cooling at the top of the troposphere, around −56 °C. Below you, the weather. Above you, a sky that only gets darker. Inside, the cabin stays comfortable throughout.

04 / 07 · ≈ 50,000 – 115,000 ft Stratosphere · Ozone layer

Inside the ozone layer.

The layer that shields Earth from ultraviolet light. Up here the air begins to warm again as you climb, and the horizon starts to curve.

05 / 07 · ≈ 62,000 ft Armstrong limit

Beyond the Armstrong limit.

Above this line, water boils at body temperature. The pressurized cabin is your world now, with environmental control systems built for total passenger comfort.

06 / 07 · ≈ 80,000 ft Black sky

Blue turns to black.

With less than 3% of the atmosphere left above you, there is too little air to scatter sunlight. The sky goes black in full daylight.

07 / 07 · ≈ 100,000 ft · 30 km The edge of space

The edge of space.

Above about 99% of Earth’s atmosphere. The curve of the planet, the thin blue line of the sky, and the black of space, all at once.
